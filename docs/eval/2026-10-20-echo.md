## FLEURS double-talk fixture

## Choosing the band

125 bands (keep 0.20 to 0.45, neighbours 0.45 to 0.65, drop 0.50 to 0.70, steps of 0.05) on meeting 12 and the fixture; the owner's words rank over residual duplicates (a dropped owner word is lost, a kept duplicate is visible noise). Of the bands that keep the fixture's WER change at or under +1.0 point and meet every bar, 0.20 / 0.50 / 0.55 drops the most duplicates; the shipped 0.20 / 0.50 / 0.60 gives up 0.07 points of them (about nine words of 12,260) for 0.93 points of WER margin on the fixture. A negative change means the gate lowers "me" WER: it removes echo words recognised inside the owner's utterances while keeping the owner's. The bands with a drop bound of 0.65 or more keep the owner's words just as well but let meeting 12's real echo through: there the echo words sit lower than the fixture's (word median 0.74, p10 0.62, against 0.98 for the fixture's pure delay), because the room spreads the echo.

Why the gate alone cannot keep every word spoken over the far end: the correlation of a word with the loopback measures how much of the mic's power over that word is the echo, about the square root of the echo's share. With the owner 5 dB over the echo the share is 1 / (1 + 10^0.5) = 0.24, so an owner word spoken over continuous far-end speech correlates at about 0.49, close to the shipped neighbour threshold, while an echo word with no owner speech sits at about 0.74 in meeting 12 (median) and 0.98 in the fixture; an echo word recognised from the mixture correlates like the owner's word beside it. The band above trades a few such words either way. Recovering them takes removing the echo from the signal before recognition, which is the canceller of plan 15 chunk B.

## Post-pass canceller (B2a)

Plan 15 chunk B2a: the processing after Stop runs Diarist's echo canceller over `mic.wav` with `loopback.wav` as the reference into a temporary `mic-aec.wav`, transcribes that instead of the raw mic, then runs the word gate over the cancelled transcript's words, scored against the raw mic. Configuration: the spike's best (research 14 section 2: the reference placed on the mic's clock through the anchors, the GCC-PHAT lag track and the 1 s phase-slope wobble; 200 ms filter, 5 ms lookahead, residual suppression), `crates/core/src/audio/aec/offline.rs`. Meeting 12 has no gain track, so its reference is the post-AGC loopback; the rows marked "gain track" replay the AGC's law backwards over it to stand in for one. `crates/core/tests/aec_postpass.rs` runs the real `run_post_pass` (EN model, lookahead 13, `v3-offline`) on a scratch database with meeting 12's clock anchors, with `echo.mode` off and auto, and scores "me" with the measurement's classes (plan 15 section 2). The off run reproduces the stored mic transcript word for word (14,627 words, every labelled word in place), so the rows compare like with like. No transcript text is reproduced here.

### Signal and the gain track

| Reference | ERLE, far end only (labelled spans) | `erle_db` (label-free) | Owner alone: level | Owner over the far end: level | Resets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post-AGC loopback (meeting 12 as stored) | 14.7 dB | 14.35 dB | −0.12 dB | −0.47 dB | 0 |
| Gain track, a value per 10 ms (the format) | 14.7 dB | 17.05 dB | −0.12 dB | −0.37 dB | 0 |
| Gain track, one value per 100 ms frame | 14.8 dB | 17.06 dB | −0.12 dB | −0.37 dB | 0 |

`erle_db` sums mic and output energy over the 2 s blocks where the loopback is active and the mic stands no more than 2 dB above the level the median return loss predicts (more is the owner talking); with the AGC undone that prediction holds on more blocks, hence the higher figure. On meeting 12 one value per frame would have done as well as one per 10 ms, because its AGC moves fast mostly while the far end is quiet; the format keeps the 10 ms values (1.44 MB per hour) so that the replay is exact whatever the far end does (the AGC moves up to 4 dB within a frame).
