## 1. The rule, as plan 18 section 3.3 fixed it before the run

**The fill.**
- The EN run is primary.
- Wherever the EN words leave a stretch of at least G seconds with no word (G in {1.0, 1.5, 2.0}), the 3.5 words whose spans lie inside that stretch, shrunk by 0.25 s at both ends, are inserted.
- 3.5's speaker labels are mapped to EN's by the maximum overlap of co-timed words outside the gaps. The mapping is an assignment over pairs of words whose spans overlap by at least 60 percent.
- A filled word takes its mapped label. A 3.5 label with no mapping takes the nearest EN word's label and is counted as `unmapped`.
- No EN word is dropped or moved.
- The fused words are scored through the file import, so the loop collapse and punctuation run as in the app. The measures come from `run.py --metrics-only`.

**Decision rule.**

1. G is the value with the lowest pooled cpWER on the gate set (7 sessions, 324 min) with EN; ties go to the smaller G.
2. With that G, the fill ships (chunk N3) only if, on the 36 Zoom and AMI sessions with EN primary:
   - pooled cpWER is lower than EN alone by at least 0.2 points;
   - no session is worse by more than 0.3;
   - AMI ES2002a's cpWER is lower;
   - gaps of 5 s or more are at least 30 percent fewer;
   - WDER on no session is worse by more than 0.5 points.
3. Confirmation, read once on the held-out public English groups: pooled cpWER not higher than EN alone, and deletions fewer. A failure here stops the chunk whatever the Zoom sessions said.
4. Default `transcript.gap_fill = auto` if the pooled gain in rule 2 is at least 0.3 points; `off` by default, offered in Settings, if it is between 0.2 and 0.3.
5. The talks (7 sessions, 356 min) are reported beside the result as the generalisation check.

**Points the plan leaves open.** These were fixed in `tools/eval/gapfill.py`'s docstring and committed (`265f4c7`) before any fill was built or scored:

- **Time base.** Times are integer microseconds, rounded as the import's `secs_to_us` rounds them.
- **Stretches.** A stretch runs from the latest word end so far to the next word start. The file's start and end count as bounds, so leading and trailing stretches are filled too.
- **"Overlap by at least 60 percent".** Each span of the pair must be at least 60 percent covered, that is, overlap ≥ 0.6 × the longer span.
- **Mapping weights.** The mapping maximises the summed overlap in microseconds, one to one (Hungarian). An assigned pair with zero weight is not a mapping.
- **"Nearest EN word".** It is the nearer of the two EN words that bound the stretch; a tie goes to the earlier word.
- **The oracle.** A stretch is filled only when 3.5's tokens there have a lower Levenshtein distance to the reference words whose midpoint lies in the stretch than leaving it empty does.

**"EN alone".** It is EN's own cached words replayed through the same import (`en_replay`). That is the beta.5 app path, the same as the fused configs apart from the fill. Section 3 shows it reproduces beta.5's release numbers.

## 4. Where the missed words are (plan 18 section 3.2)

`levers.py gaps-why` takes every gap of 2 s or more of each model, counted exactly as `metrics.py` counts them. The totals equal the baselines: EN 826 / 65 and 3.5 889 / 88 on the 36 sessions; EN 1,190 / 315 and 3.5 1,207 / 259 on the public 52. For each gap it records:

- **Level.** The audio's RMS over the gap, against the session's median speech level: the median of the 100 ms frame levels inside the reference RTTM's speech, 16 kHz mono.
- **Overlap.** The number of distinct reference speakers among the gap's reference words, and the time with two or more reference speakers active at once.
- **The other model.** Its words whose span lies inside the gap shrunk by 0.25 s, which is what a fill could insert. How many of them align as equal with the gap's reference words (time-ordered, speaker-agnostic Levenshtein; in overlapped speech the interleaved reference undercounts this). And whether the other model's own gaps cover at least half of this one.

Cause classes, in this order:
- **quiet:** more than 12 dB under the session's median speech level;
- **overlapped:** two or more reference speakers among its words;
- **other.**

Zoom references serialise overlap into consecutive cues and drop short backchannels, so "overlapped" there means a turn change inside the gap. Two speakers at once can only be read on the human references.

What the characterisation says, measured:

- **Quiet speech explains almost none of the gaps.**
  - 23 of EN's 826 gaps on the 36 sessions are quiet (107 of 3,274 reference words), and 8 of 1,190 on the public sets.
  - The median gap is at the session's speech level (+0.6 dB on the Zoom and AMI sessions, +3.3 dB on the public ones).
  - Inference: a gain stage would not reach these gaps, whatever chunk N2's input-gain report finds on FLEURS.
- **On the public sets the gaps are overlapped speech.**
  - 966 of EN's 1,190 gaps (81 percent, 12,412 of 14,449 reference words) hold words of two or more reference speakers.
  - 1,021 have two speakers talking at once for 0.2 s or more.
  - On the Zoom references only 31 of 826 have a turn change inside.
- **The two models mostly miss the same stretches.**
  - 3.5's own gaps cover half or more of 66 percent of EN's gaps on the Zoom and AMI sessions, and of 59 percent on the public sets.
  - 3.5 has any word inside only 17 percent of EN's Zoom and AMI gaps (144 of 826).
  - On the public sets 3.5 has words inside 55 percent of EN's gaps, and in 68 percent of EN's gaps of 5 s or more.
- **What a fill can reach on the 36 sessions is small.** Over all of EN's gaps, 3.5's words inside them align with 144 reference words, against EN's 6,350 deletions over the 36 sessions.

- **3.5 alone reproduces beta.5's 3.5 rows.** cpWER, tcpWER, WDER, DER, count error and both gap counts are equal on all 36 sessions; pooled cpWER is 11.30. Five sessions differ by a few words in del and ins only, for the reason section 3 gives.
- **The oracle is not better than EN alone either** (10.86). Here a stretch is filled only where 3.5's words beat an empty stretch against the reference words in it. The choice is per stretch and speaker-agnostic, as fixed before the run, so it bounds the choice of stretches at this G, not the speakers or the alignment. Even so, choosing stretches by the references does not bring the fill near the 0.2-point bar on these sessions. On AMI ES2002a the oracle and the fill are equal (16.73).
- **3.5 with EN filling** gains 0.10 points over 3.5 alone (11.30 → 11.20) and halves its gaps of 5 s or more (88 → 42). It stays above EN alone.

## 6. Verdict under the rule

**Closed.** Rule 2 fails two of its bars at the G that rule 1 picked:
- the pooled cpWER gain is −0.02 points against a bar of +0.2;
- gaps of 5 s or more fall 23.1 percent against a bar of 30.

Rule 2 is a condition for shipping, so the fill does not ship. Rule 3's pass does not reopen it: the rule makes the public confirmation a further condition, never a substitute for rule 2.

What follows from the rule:
- Chunk N3 does not start, and plan 18 section 12's freeze item reads "N1 closed".
- `transcript.gap_fill` is not added.
- The harness keeps the replay configs. They cost nothing to run again.

## 7. What the numbers say beside the verdict

These are observations for the planner. Nothing here re-reads the rule.

- **The two kinds of reference disagree.**
  - The Zoom references are Zoom's own recogniser. On them the fill is neutral to slightly harmful: the 35 Zoom sessions alone go from 10.78 to 10.81 (+81 cp errors), and their gaps of 5 s or more from 61 to 49.
  - AMI ES2002a is the one human-referenced session among the 36. It gains 1.30 points (18.03 → 16.73), and its gaps of 5 s or more go from 4 to 1.
  - On the 52 public sessions, all with human references, the fill gains 1.06 points pooled. pub-meeting gains 1.41 points and pub-far 1.24; pub-phone moves by +0.04. Gaps of 5 s or more halve.
  - Inference: Zoom's recogniser misses much of the same speech, so on Zoom references 3.5's extra words count as insertions where people would have counted them as recovered.
- **Where the fill acts.** Inference, from section 4: the fill's gain on the public sets comes from the overlapped stretches, where 3.5 has words in 55 percent of EN's gaps and in 68 percent of the long ones. Measured: on the owner's Zoom recordings EN's gaps are seldom overlapped, and 3.5 has words in only 17 percent of them.
- **Smaller G is worse on the gate set** (+0.12 at 1.0 s, +0.05 at 1.5 s). Inference: short stretches between words are pauses more often than missed speech.
- **Speakers cost little.** Every filled word took a mapped label except on one session per set: Talk 7 (16 words) and one public session (1 word). WDER rises by at most 0.10 on any Zoom or AMI session, and by 0.22 pooled on the public sets (0.75 on pub-far).
- **If this is ever revisited**, it is a new decision with a rule fixed before a new run. Human-referenced data the fill has not been measured on would be the natural place, since the public sets here are held out and have now been read once. That is the planner's and the owner's call.

## 8. Reproducing

From `tools/eval` with the README setup:
- `DIARIST_EVAL_CLI` names a diarist-cli built from this tree.
- `DIARIST_EVAL_MODELS_DIR` names a models folder holding the punctuation entry's files under its `dir`.
- The diarist-core test binary comes from `cargo test --release --locked -p diarist-core --lib --no-run`; set `DIARIST_GAPFILL_TEST_BIN` if it is not the newest in `target/release/deps`.

```sh
  uv run python -I gapfill.py --sessions-file $PRIV run --config $c --groups tuning,heldout,control,stress,overlap \
      --source en=$ZRUNS/rs_en_shipped --source n35=$ZRUNS/rs_n35_shipped
  uv run python -I run.py --config $c --sessions-file $PRIV --groups tuning,heldout,control,stress,overlap --metrics-only --no-report

```
